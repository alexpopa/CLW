# Colonial Line Wars
## Game Mechanics Research & Standalone Recreation Design Specification

**Research date:** November 12, 2026  
**Reference:** Colonial Line Wars, StarCraft II Arcade  
**Objective:** Develop a fully playable standalone strategy game inspired by Colonial Line Wars, preserving its core systems, decision-making, and balance philosophy.

---

# PART I — UNDERSTANDING THE ORIGINAL GAME

## 1. Game Overview

Colonial Line Wars is a real-time, multiplayer, lane-based strategy game originally created as a custom StarCraft II map.

The original game is credited to Lithe, while an Arcade version is credited to Toppert. A newer version published by LazyAss received an update on November 7, 2026.

The standard game mode is **3 versus 3**, with two teams competing to destroy the opposing team's colony.

Unlike traditional RTS games, players generally do not control their armies directly. Instead, they construct buildings that automatically generate units at regular intervals. Those units move along predefined lanes, fight enemies, capture strategic territory, and eventually attack the opposing base.

The player's primary responsibility is making strategic decisions rather than micromanaging individual units.

The core decisions are:

1. Which units should be produced?
2. Where should their production buildings be placed?
3. How much should be invested into future income?
4. When should stronger units be researched?
5. Which counters are needed against the opposing army?
6. When should special abilities be used?
7. When should resources be spent helping another lane?

The resulting experience combines elements of a real-time strategy game, an autobattler, an economy-management game, and a tug-of-war.

**Central design principle:** Players should win through stronger decisions about economy, army composition, positioning, and timing—not by clicking faster.

## 2. Battlefield and Map Layout

The battlefield contains three parallel lanes, or canyons, connecting the two opposing colonies.

The teams begin at opposite ends of the battlefield.

The lanes are:

- Top lane
- Middle lane
- Bottom lane

Each lane contains three resource beacons, for a total of nine beacons across the map.

The map is conceptually organized as:

| Western colony | Three contested lanes | Eastern colony |
|---|---|---|
| Allied production area | Top: Beacon 1 → Beacon 2 → Beacon 3 | Enemy production area |
| Allied production area | Middle: Beacon 1 → Beacon 2 → Beacon 3 | Enemy production area |
| Allied production area | Bottom: Beacon 1 → Beacon 2 → Beacon 3 | Enemy production area |

In the standard 3v3 format, each team has one player principally responsible for each lane.

However, team coordination is important. Players can contribute units to allied lanes rather than exclusively defending their own.

The lanes are relatively separate. Their battles and resource-control states influence the entire team's economy.

### Design implications

The geography creates several distinct strategic situations:

**Evenly contested lane:** Armies fight around the central beacon, with neither team maintaining a lasting advantage.

**Winning lane:** One side pushes beyond the midpoint, captures additional beacons, and begins earning more resources.

**Losing lane:** The defending player loses beacon income while facing increasing pressure from enemy reinforcements.

**Collapsed lane:** Enemy units reach the defending colony. Teammates may need to redirect resources and military production to prevent defeat.

This creates a natural relationship between tactical success and economic advantage.

## 3. Building-Based Unit Production

The defining feature of Colonial Line Wars is that **units are generated by permanent buildings**.

Players do not purchase individual units for every attack.

Instead, they purchase a production building once. That building repeatedly produces a designated unit.

A historical description specifies a production interval of approximately **30 seconds**.

For example:

A player builds five Marine production buildings.

Every production cycle, five Marines are produced.

After five cycles, the player will have generated 25 Marines, assuming each building remained operational.

The player's initial building investment therefore generates military value repeatedly.

### Why this is important

This creates a compounding military economy.

A building is not merely a purchase of one soldier. It is a recurring source of soldiers for the remainder of the match.

Players must consequently evaluate:

- Upfront building cost
- Unit strength
- Expected building lifespan
- Number of future production cycles
- How well the generated units work together
- Whether the opponent can efficiently counter them

A weak but inexpensive unit can be valuable if purchased early enough to generate many waves.

A strong expensive unit can be valuable if its effectiveness compensates for fewer production buildings.

### Building location and formations

The current Arcade description explicitly states that units move in the formation created by the player.

Therefore building placement should matter.

The spatial relationship between production buildings should be reflected in the spawning units.

A recommended recreation would allow:

- Durable melee units in front
- Ranged attackers behind
- Healers behind or among protected units
- Siege weapons near the rear
- Air units positioned according to their preferred attack ranges

The formation should be preserved as much as possible during initial movement, but actual combat, different movement speeds, unit collision, and targeting should naturally disrupt it.

This is essential. Replacing formations with random spawning would remove a meaningful part of the game.

## 4. Autonomous Movement and Combat

Once produced, units move toward the opposing colony automatically.

Players should not need to issue movement or attack commands.

The unit AI handles:

- Moving along the assigned lane
- Locating appropriate enemy targets
- Attacking enemies in range
- Closing distance when necessary
- Using automatic abilities
- Continuing toward the enemy colony when no enemies remain

Combat is continuous rather than turn-based.

An important feature is that units can survive beyond the production cycle in which they spawned.

Surviving units can combine with the next wave of reinforcements, producing increasingly large armies.

This creates the characteristic tug-of-war effect.

### Example

At 30 seconds, Team A produces six units and Team B produces five.

Team A wins the battle with two survivors.

At 60 seconds, another six Team A units arrive.

Team A may now have eight units confronting Team B's next five.

Unless Team B changes its composition or adds production, Team A's advantage can grow.

This also means that combat effectiveness cannot be judged solely by comparing two isolated units.

A unit's ability to survive and join later waves matters substantially.

## 5. Resource Beacon Control

Each lane contains three resource beacons.

A beacon's purpose is to provide recurring economic income.

The confirmed control mechanism is straightforward:

**Moving a unit across a beacon captures it for that unit's team.**

Control does not require a worker, special capture order, or separate building.

The capture mechanic is directly connected to how far an army manages to push along its lane.

A team that wins battles naturally advances and captures more beacons.

A team that loses battles naturally retreats and loses them.

The original description indicates that ownership depends on which team's units touched the checkpoint most recently.

### Why three beacons per lane matter

Three beacons create several recognizable territorial states.

| Beacons controlled | Strategic state |
|---|---|
| 0 | Complete loss of territorial control |
| 1 | Defensive or disadvantaged position |
| 2 | Favorable territorial control |
| 3 | Dominant control of the lane |

This is more interesting than a single control point.

A player can be winning without completely overpowering the enemy.

It also enables income investment decisions to depend on the degree of territorial control.

## 6. Economy and Lane Investment

The economy is arguably the game's most distinctive mechanic.

Players receive resources periodically.

Income is affected by:

1. Base income.
2. The number of resource beacons their team controls.
3. Additional investments made in individual lanes.

Players can invest their money into a specific lane to increase the income generated by the beacons controlled there.

This produces an important tradeoff:

**Money spent investing is money temporarily unavailable for building an army.**

An investment only creates value over time.

If the player spends too aggressively on income, they may lose the battles necessary to preserve that income.

### The most important investment rule

The current Arcade instructions explicitly advise players that investing in a lane is generally worthwhile only when their team controls **at least two of its three beacons**.

This is fundamental to the intended strategy.

A player who controls only one beacon receives too little additional income to reliably justify an investment.

A player controlling two or three beacons receives more benefit.

### Economic strategy example

Suppose a player controls three beacons in the top lane.

They could spend their next 200 resources on another production building.

That would strengthen their army immediately.

Alternatively, they could invest those 200 resources into top-lane income.

That increases future income but leaves their current army unchanged.

If they maintain control of the beacons, they may eventually earn enough extra resources to construct multiple additional buildings.

But if the opponent counters their army and captures the beacons, the investment becomes much less valuable.

This creates a balancing relationship between military strength and economic growth.

### Critical recreation requirement

Income investments must remain tied to actual territorial control.

Do not replace this with generic passive income upgrades that work equally well regardless of the battlefield.

The original game's strategy depends on investing in positions that the player can realistically maintain.

## 7. Technology Research and Unit Tiers

Players begin with access to relatively basic units.

More advanced units must be unlocked through a research facility.

Research consumes resources and takes time.

The original game organizes units into four broad tiers.

### Tier 1 — Basic units

Historical examples include:

- Marine
- Zergling
- Zealot
- Reaper
- Hydralisk

Tier 1 units provide the foundation of early armies.

They are relatively inexpensive and can be produced in larger numbers.

Their value comes from early availability, affordability, and appropriate counters.

### Tier 2 — Specialized units

This tier adds more specialized or powerful units.

They may offer:

- Improved survivability
- Better anti-armor performance
- Area damage
- Healing or support
- Greater range
- More specialized targeting

The exact complete Tier 2 roster was not verified from the available sources.

### Tier 3 — Advanced units

Historical examples explicitly identified as Tier 3 include:

- Siege Tank
- Void Ray

These units create powerful tactical advantages but require substantial investment in technology and production.

### Tier 4 — Elite units

The historical game granted each player one gas resource that could be spent on one Tier 4 unit-production option.

Documented examples include:

- Carrier
- Battlecruiser
- Archon
- Infestor
- Colossus

The one-gas mechanic is particularly important.

It prevents players from simply constructing every available endgame unit.

Players must choose where to commit their limited Tier 4 capability.

### Research tradeoff

Research costs money and takes time, while existing units continue fighting.

A player who researches too early can become militarily weak.

A player who researches too late risks becoming unable to counter an opponent's advanced army.

This makes technology timing a central part of the strategy.

### Team specialization

Because different players can pursue different research paths, teammates can specialize.

One player can emphasize cheap early pressure.

Another can invest in advanced technology.

A third can build specialized counter-units or reinforce a struggling lane.

The game should reward these complementary strategies.

## 8. Units and Counterplay

Colonial Line Wars uses unit matchups rather than simply making every higher-tier unit universally stronger.

A higher-tier unit should generally offer greater power, but it should still have weaknesses.

Examples of important combat relationships include:

**Massed light infantry versus splash damage:** A large army of inexpensive infantry may defeat ordinary opponents through concentrated damage. However, a unit capable of hitting multiple infantry simultaneously can counter that investment efficiently.

**Durable frontline units versus ranged attackers:** Tough melee units protect more fragile ranged units by absorbing damage.

**Air units versus ground-only units:** An army lacking anti-air capability can be overwhelmed by flying units.

**Specialized anti-air versus expensive air units:** Investing in air units should not guarantee victory when the opponent responds appropriately.

**Healing and support versus burst damage:** Sustained healing may dominate low-damage opponents but perform poorly against concentrated damage or splash attacks.

**Long-range siege versus short-range armies:** Siege units can inflict substantial damage before the opposing army reaches them, but may struggle when fast units close the distance.

**Area damage versus clustered formations:** Grouped units become vulnerable to attacks affecting multiple targets.

These relationships form a counter system.

They should not be entirely rigid. A player with significantly more resources invested into an army should often beat a technically favorable counter with much less investment.

### Original unit references

Patch notes and historical documentation also mention the following units, although their complete current tier placements and statistics were not confirmed:

- Firebat
- Marauder
- Ghost
- Medic
- Goliath
- Viking
- Roach/Ravenger
- Stalker
- Dark Templar
- Sentry
- Scout

These can be used as references for designing equivalent original unit archetypes.

## 9. Special Abilities and Emergency Support

A historical account describes several player-activated battlefield abilities.

### Wraith Run

A player could spend resources to send a Wraith through a selected lane, dropping bombs on enemy forces.

Strategic purpose:

- Reinforce an active push
- Damage clustered enemies
- Assist a losing lane
- Create a temporary advantage without permanently purchasing more production buildings

### Spore Salvo

A player could spend resources to attack visible enemy forces with acid.

Strategic purpose:

- Weaken armies from a distance
- Counter grouped units
- Support an allied lane
- Turn an otherwise unfavorable engagement

### Plea for Help

The historical account also describes a limited emergency ability that destroyed units within a lane.

Each player had one such emergency use.

This effectively served as an emergency reset against a dangerous enemy push.

For a recreation, the exact reset strength should be considered carefully, because an unrestricted army-clearing ability can erase a large strategic advantage.

These abilities should be treated as expensive interventions, not ordinary attacks that players constantly spam.

**Version caveat:** These are documented historical mechanics. Their exact costs, cooldowns, and availability in the November 2026 version have not been independently confirmed.

## 10. Victory Condition

The objective is to destroy the opposing team's colony base.

Successful lane pressure eventually allows units to reach the enemy's end of the battlefield.

Those surviving units attack the opposing colony.

The game ends when the colony is destroyed.

The gameplay loop is therefore:

Build army → Win battles → Capture beacons → Improve income → Expand or upgrade army → Push into enemy territory → Destroy colony.

However, winning territory is not sufficient by itself.

A player must eventually turn economic and military advantages into decisive offensive pressure.

## 11. Recent November 2026 Update

The version published by LazyAss includes changes dated November 7, 2026.

The published changes include:

| Unit | Documented change |
|---|---|
| Sentry | Guardian Shield automatically activates when an enemy is within range 6. It lasts 10 seconds and recharges for 5 seconds. |
| Dark Templar | When killed, creates a spectral echo with half its health and shields. |
| Roach/Ravenger | Roach is replaced by Ravenger, which adds a corrosive attack dealing 20 damage every 10 seconds. |
| Stalker | Blinking creates a decoy that attracts enemy attacks for 3 seconds without dealing damage. |
| Firebat | Flame attacks ignite affected units for 2 damage per second over 4 seconds. |

The update also added notifications and lane pings when players leave, improved graphical presentation, and fixed missing sell controls for certain buildings, including Tier 4 buildings.

These changes demonstrate a useful balance philosophy:

**Give individual units distinctive tactical functions rather than relying exclusively on raw damage and health increases.**

Older patch notes also show repeated fine-tuning of attack periods, range, health, damage, and research costs.

For example, the historical balance records include changes to Marine, Roach, Stalker, Zealot, Dark Templar, Viking, Goliath, Siege Tank, and Void Ray.

This suggests that small changes to attack frequency or attack range can meaningfully affect the strength of production-based armies.

# PART II — HOW THE GAME IS BALANCED

## 12. The Four Core Balancing Systems

Colonial Line Wars should not be balanced around simple one-on-one unit fights.

It is balanced through interacting systems.

### A. Military investment versus economic investment

Every resource spent on income is a resource not spent on immediate military power.

The short-term advantage belongs to the player investing in combat.

The long-term advantage may belong to the player successfully investing in income.

Neither strategy should always win.

### B. Territorial control versus income growth

A player must win battles to control resource beacons.

A player must control resource beacons to fully benefit from economic investments.

However, overinvesting in income can cause a player to lose those battles.

This feedback loop is the core of the economy.

### C. Army composition versus unit counters

No unit type should efficiently counter every other unit type.

Players should continually evaluate what their opponent is producing.

A player who ignores enemy composition should be vulnerable even when spending the same amount of money.

### D. Current military strength versus future technology

Research requires spending resources without receiving an immediate increase in army size.

The player who postpones research can generate a larger short-term army.

The player who researches successfully may gain a long-term composition advantage.

Good players must judge when their existing force is strong enough to support a technology investment.

## 13. Important Balance Principles

The following principles should guide a recreation.

### Rule 1: Expensive does not mean universally superior

A powerful siege unit should defeat clustered infantry efficiently but should not automatically defeat inexpensive fast units that reach it.

### Rule 2: Every strategy needs counterplay

Mass infantry, air rushes, siege compositions, elite-unit strategies, and heavy investment strategies must all have meaningful counters.

### Rule 3: Positioning should matter

Even if two players spend identical resources, the player with a better formation should often win.

### Rule 4: Economy should reward territorial strength

A player who consistently maintains control of two or three beacons should be able to profit from investing.

A player unable to maintain territory should find aggressive investments risky.

### Rule 5: Elite units should influence, not instantly decide, the outcome

Tier 4 should feel powerful without rendering mixed lower-tier compositions obsolete.

### Rule 6: Teammates must be able to assist each other

A struggling lane should not become hopeless simply because its assigned player has fallen slightly behind.

Other players should be able to contribute production and tactical support.

### Rule 7: Large armies should remain vulnerable

An accumulated army should be threatening, but area damage, specialized counters, and other appropriate mechanics must offer ways to break through.

### Rule 8: The losing team should retain meaningful decisions

Territorial advantages are desirable, but the game should avoid an economic spiral in which defeat becomes mathematically inevitable too early.

## 14. Known Balance Problems in the Historical Game

Historical player accounts identify several problems worth avoiding.

**Player departures:** Older accounts describe cases where departures distorted team income or made games frustratingly uneven.

**Poor team coordination:** Because lanes are interconnected strategically, one player making severe mistakes can undermine an otherwise strong team.

**Runaway advantages:** Surviving armies can accumulate while the winning side also captures more income-generating territory.

**Excessive army accumulation:** Large numbers of surviving units can become difficult to counter and cause performance problems.

**Dominant build orders:** If a particular unit composition has too few counters, players can repeatedly use it without adapting.

A successful recreation should preserve the strategic consequences of a losing lane while reducing frustration caused by these issues.

# PART III — PROPOSED STANDALONE GAME DESIGN

The remainder of this document provides an **original, implementable design inspired by Colonial Line Wars**.

Numerical values are proposed starting values, not verified statistics from the original map.

The objective is to reproduce the core strategic experience while allowing independent balancing and development.

## 15. Working Concept

**Working title:** Frontier Lines

**Genre:** Real-time lane strategy / autobattler / tug-of-war

**Perspective:** Top-down or slightly angled 2D

**Primary mode:** 3v3

**Additional modes:** 1v1, 2v2, and single-player versus AI

**Target platform:** Desktop web browser initially

**Target match duration:** Approximately 15–25 minutes

**Player control:** Building placement, research, income investment, special abilities, and strategic decisions.

**No direct unit micromanagement.**

### Factions

Create three original sci-fi factions inspired by broad RTS archetypes:

**Industrial Coalition**
- Durable troops
- Firearms and artillery
- Healing and mechanical support
- Strong ranged firepower

**Brood Collective**
- Fast biological units
- Aggressive frontline pressure
- Regeneration and damage-over-time effects
- Efficient mass-unit compositions

**Aether Dominion**
- Energy shields
- Mobility and teleportation
- Defensive support abilities
- High-tech specialized units

Each faction should be competitive but feel different to play.

Use original character designs, artwork, sounds, names, and interfaces rather than copying StarCraft II assets.

## 16. Proposed Map Specifications

The map contains three horizontal combat lanes.

Each lane begins in one team's production area and ends in the opposing team's production area.

Use a mirrored, symmetrical layout so neither team receives a positional advantage.

### Required map elements

- Two colony headquarters
- Three parallel lanes
- Nine resource beacons
- Production-building zones at both ends
- Research interfaces
- Lane-investment interfaces
- Visible wave timers
- Clear territorial ownership indicators
- Distinct team colors

### Suggested dimensions

Use a logical battlefield length of 240 world units.

Each lane should be approximately 24–30 world units wide.

Place the three beacons at approximately:

- 25% of the distance along the lane
- 50% of the distance along the lane
- 75% of the distance along the lane

Movement and spacing should be adjusted during playtesting.

### Lane interaction

Every player has a default lane but may build production structures in another allied lane.

A player's army always belongs to their original team, regardless of the lane where it was produced.

This allows coordinated pushes and assistance.

## 17. Proposed Economy

Use one main currency called Credits.

Income occurs every 10 seconds.

### Starting values

| Setting | Proposed value |
|---|---:|
| Starting credits per player | 350 |
| Base income per player | 32 credits / 10 sec |
| Basic beacon income | 4 credits / 10 sec |
| Resource beacons per lane | 3 |
| Number of lanes | 3 |
| Income interval | 10 seconds |

All players receive their own base income.

Each player's beacon income uses the beacons controlled by their team.

Investments are purchased individually by players, even though beacon ownership is shared by the team.

### Income formula

For each player:

**Income per tick = Base income + Σ [controlled beacons in lane × (basic beacon value + personal lane-investment bonus)]**

Calculate the sum across all three lanes.

### Proposed lane investment tiers

| Investment level | Upgrade cost | Additional income per controlled beacon per 10 sec |
|---|---:|---:|
| Level 0 | Free | +0 |
| Level 1 | 180 | +5 |
| Level 2 | 300 | +8 |
| Level 3 | 480 | +12 |
| Level 4 | 700 | +16 |

These are incremental bonuses and costs.

For example, Level 2 provides a cumulative bonus of 13 credits per controlled beacon per tick.

### Investment return example

For the first investment, the bonus is 5 credits per controlled beacon every 10 seconds.

- One controlled beacon: +5/tick; approximate payback 360 seconds.
- Two controlled beacons: +10/tick; approximate payback 180 seconds.
- Three controlled beacons: +15/tick; approximate payback 120 seconds.

This recreates the original game's strategic guidance that investing becomes attractive when controlling at least two beacons.

### Important economic restrictions

- Investments do not generate income from enemy-controlled beacons.
- Investments do not generate income from neutral beacons.
- Beacon capture changes income eligibility immediately.
- Investment levels remain purchased even if a player loses territory.
- Investments should not be freely refundable.
- No additional kill-bounty income is included in the initial implementation.
- No unrelated passive income upgrades should replace lane-specific investments.

## 18. Production Building System

Each production building represents one recurring unit source.

### Core rules

1. Players pay credits to construct a building.
2. Buildings are placed inside valid allied production zones.
3. The building becomes active after construction completes.
4. Every 30 seconds, active buildings create their assigned units.
5. Units spawn in formations based on building positions.
6. Units automatically move toward the enemy.
7. Buildings continue generating units until sold or otherwise removed.

### Production waves

Use one synchronized global wave timer.

All eligible buildings spawn simultaneously at the end of each cycle.

This makes formations easier to understand and makes strategic timing more important.

### Proposed timings

| Setting | Value |
|---|---:|
| Production interval | 30 seconds |
| Standard construction time | 2 seconds |
| First production wave | 30 seconds after match start |
| Building placement | Grid or snap-assisted |
| Building selling | Supported |

### Selling buildings

For the initial implementation:

- Before a building's first production cycle: refund 75% of its cost.
- After it has produced units: refund 25% of its cost.
- Research costs are never refunded.
- Tier 4 production tokens are returned when the associated Tier 4 building is sold.
- Selling cannot reverse a completed production wave.

These rules reduce repeated buying and selling exploits.

## 19. Unit Attributes and Combat System

Every unit should have the following attributes:

- Unit ID and name
- Faction
- Tier
- Building cost
- Health
- Shields, if applicable
- Armor
- Damage
- Attack interval
- Effective DPS
- Attack range
- Movement speed
- Target eligibility: ground, air, or both
- Attack type
- Splash radius, if applicable
- Status effects
- Special abilities
- Ability cooldowns
- Collision radius
- Preferred formation position

Use deterministic simulation whenever practical.

### Suggested combat mechanics

**Health:** Damage removes health until the unit dies.

**Shields:** Absorb damage before health and may regenerate if the relevant unit is designed to do so.

**Armor:** Reduces damage using a transparent rule. A reasonable initial formula is:

Damage taken = max(1, attack damage − armor).

**Attack range:** Determines how close a unit must be before attacking.

**Movement speed:** Influences positioning, time to contact, and frontline stability.

**Splash damage:** Damages multiple units in an area.

**Ground and air targeting:** A unit can only attack target types explicitly supported by its weapon.

**Healing:** Restores health but does not revive dead units unless explicitly designed to do so.

**Status effects:** May include burning, corrosion, slowing, shielding, taunting, or temporary distraction.

### Target-selection behavior

Default combat AI should:

1. Identify valid enemy targets in detection range.
2. Prefer the nearest valid enemy.
3. Move into range if necessary.
4. Attack when the weapon cooldown permits.
5. Reevaluate if the target dies or becomes invalid.
6. Continue advancing if no targets remain.

Healing units should prioritize injured allied units within range.

Taunts should temporarily modify target selection.

Units must not repeatedly switch targets every simulation frame without a meaningful reason.

## 21. Proposed Faction Abilities

Every faction should have at least two meaningful unit mechanics beyond ordinary attacks.

### Industrial Coalition

**Incendiary Assault**
- Cone-shaped flame attack.
- Applies burning damage.
- Effective against clustered infantry.

**Field Treatment**
- Medic periodically heals an injured allied unit.
- Healing must not affect dead units.
- Healing efficiency should be weaker against burst damage.

**Siege Bombardment**
- Long-range area damage.
- Effective against densely packed groups.
- Vulnerable to fast units once they close the distance.

### Brood Collective

**Regenerative Feeding**
- Certain units regain health after dealing damage.
- Encourages sustained combat.

**Corrosive Bile**
- Periodically attacks an area.
- Damages multiple enemies.
- Particularly useful against compact formations.

**Brood Summoning**
- A specialized unit creates temporary small allied creatures.
- Summons have limited lifetimes and do not themselves spawn additional creatures.

### Aether Dominion

**Guardian Barrier**
- Periodically reduces damage received by nearby allies.
- Has a fixed duration and cooldown.

**Phase Decoy**
- A teleporting unit temporarily creates a decoy.
- The decoy attracts attacks.
- It deals no damage and expires after a short period.

**Spectral Echo**
- A designated elite or specialist unit produces a temporary weaker echo on death.
- The echo has reduced health and damage and cannot create additional echoes.

These abilities draw inspiration from documented Colonial Line Wars mechanics but should be implemented with independently balanced statistics.

## 22. Technology Research

The initial recreation should use a straightforward three-step global technology progression per player.

Tier 1 is available at match start.

| Unlock | Research cost | Research time |
|---|---:|---:|
| Tier 2 | 400 | 30 seconds |
| Tier 3 | 800 | 45 seconds |
| Tier 4 | 1,200 | 60 seconds |

Unlocks must occur sequentially.

Research progress should be visible.

Players cannot place buildings for a tier until that tier has been unlocked.

Research applies individually to each player rather than automatically to the entire team.

### Tier 4 restrictions

Each player receives one elite-production token, analogous to the historical one-gas limit.

- Only one Tier 4 production building may be active per player.
- Tier 4 requires completed research.
- The elite token is consumed when construction begins.
- Selling the building returns the token.
- A player cannot maintain two elite production buildings simultaneously.

This creates a meaningful final technology decision.

## 23. Proposed Player Abilities

Include three manually activated abilities.

### Bombing Run

- Cost: 250 credits
- Cooldown: 150 seconds
- Target: Selected lane
- Effect: A visible aircraft crosses the lane and drops several explosive attacks.
- Enemies should receive a short visual warning.
- Intended to weaken clustered enemies or support a push.

### Acid Barrage

- Cost: 190 credits
- Cooldown: 120 seconds
- Target: Visible area within a lane
- Effect: Damages enemies over several seconds.
- Useful against tightly grouped forces.
- Should not destroy elite units by itself.

### Emergency Intervention

- Uses: Once per player per match
- Target: One selected lane
- Effect: Eliminates enemy non-elite units in the targeted lane and heavily damages elite units.
- Does not damage buildings or the enemy colony.
- Includes a short activation warning.

The historical version reportedly had a more comprehensive lane-clearing emergency ability. The proposed partial protection for elites is a balance adjustment.

## 24. Proposed Counter System

The goal is to give every major composition a practical weakness.

| Enemy strategy | Effective response | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Mass cheap infantry | Splash damage | Hits multiple targets |
| Heavy frontline units | Specialized damage and ranged support | Focuses durable enemies |
| Large aircraft formations | Anti-air units | Attacks flying targets efficiently |
| Long-range siege | Fast assault units | Closes distance before repeated bombardment |
| Healing-heavy formations | Burst or concentrated damage | Overwhelms healing rate |
| Fast melee swarms | Durable frontline and area damage | Prevents easy access to fragile units |
| Expensive elite units | Specialized counters plus supporting army | Avoids inefficient head-on fighting |
| Compact ranged groups | Area damage or well-supported frontline | Punishes clustering |
| Heavy economic investment | Early military pressure | Exploits reduced immediate defenses |

Counter advantages should be meaningful without becoming absolute.

### Target balance thresholds

For armies with equal total building investment:

- Neutral matchups should be reasonably close.
- Appropriate counter-compositions should often achieve a 15–35% combat-efficiency advantage.
- A single unit type should not dominate every equal-cost opposing composition.
- Elite units should still require appropriate supporting forces.
- A player significantly ahead economically should generally be able to exploit that advantage.

These percentages are design targets, not measurements of the original game.

## 25. Preventing Runaway Advantages

The recreation should preserve the importance of winning lanes while avoiding irreversible games too early.

Suggested protections:

**Reliable base income:** Even a player with no controlled beacons must continue receiving enough income to build counters.

**No direct economy destruction:** Enemy units should not permanently eliminate a player's income-generating structures.

**Effective area damage:** Large accumulated armies must remain vulnerable to appropriate counters.

**Defensive positioning:** The defending side should have enough room and time to strengthen its next production wave.

**Emergency abilities:** A limited defensive intervention can provide temporary relief.

**Reasonable map travel times:** Units should not reach and destroy the enemy colony immediately after a single successful early fight.

**Team assistance:** Allies should be able to reinforce another lane.

**No artificial winner selection:** The losing team should not receive invisible combat buffs merely because it is losing.

Comebacks should come from player decisions rather than arbitrary rubber-banding.

## 26. Match End and Stalemate Handling

Each team should begin with a colony headquarters.

Suggested starting headquarters health: **3,000 HP**.

The headquarters should have a weak defensive weapon capable of attacking ground and air units, primarily to prevent isolated survivors from causing disproportionate damage.

It should not be strong enough to stop a genuinely superior army.

A team wins when the opposing headquarters reaches zero health.

For unusually long games, an optional sudden-death mechanic can activate at 30 minutes.

Possible sudden-death effects:

- Reduced headquarters defenses
- Slowly increasing damage received by headquarters
- A visible countdown explaining the change

Do not silently change balance rules during the match.

# PART V — ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

## 29. Single-Player AI

The game should support matches against computer-controlled opponents.

AI players must follow the same resource, research, and building rules as human players.

Do not give AI players hidden resources or automatic combat bonuses at normal difficulty.

### AI decision cycle

Every few seconds, an AI player should:

1. Evaluate its lane's battlefield position.
2. Identify the enemy's dominant unit types.
3. Assess whether it has appropriate counters.
4. Determine whether to add military production.
5. Determine whether lane investment is worthwhile.
6. Decide whether to research.
7. Consider helping an allied lane.
8. Consider using a special ability.

### Suggested AI priorities

**Emergency defense:** If enemy forces threaten the headquarters, prioritize immediate defensive counters.

**Counter building:** If enemy composition is strongly favored against the AI's army, choose appropriate counter-units.

**Economy:** If two or three beacons are consistently controlled and military pressure is manageable, consider income investment.

**Research:** If the army is sufficiently stable, invest in more advanced technology.

**Offensive reinforcement:** If the opponent is weak, expand production to exploit the advantage.

**Team support:** Reinforce allied lanes that are close to collapse or where a coordinated push could win.

### Difficulty levels

**Easy**
- Slower decisions
- Limited counter awareness
- Less efficient investments

**Normal**
- Reasonable counters
- Basic economic planning
- Occasional team assistance

**Hard**
- More efficient research timing
- Better unit-composition analysis
- Stronger investment decisions
- Coordinated cross-lane reinforcement

Difficulty should primarily affect decision quality, not resource cheating.

## 31. Simulation Requirements

Use a fixed-step simulation.

A reasonable starting point is 20 simulation updates per second.

Rendering can run independently at a higher frame rate.

The gameplay simulation must be deterministic when given the same initial state, random seed, and sequence of player commands.

This is important for:

- Multiplayer synchronization
- Debugging
- Replays
- Automated testing
- Unit-balance experiments

### Suggested events

- `MATCH_STARTED`
- `BUILDING_PLACED`
- `BUILDING_SOLD`
- `RESEARCH_STARTED`
- `RESEARCH_COMPLETED`
- `INVESTMENT_PURCHASED`
- `INCOME_DISTRIBUTED`
- `WAVE_SPAWNED`
- `UNIT_ATTACKED`
- `UNIT_DAMAGED`
- `UNIT_DIED`
- `BEACON_CAPTURED`
- `ABILITY_ACTIVATED`
- `HEADQUARTERS_DAMAGED`
- `MATCH_ENDED`

Keep unit and economic configurations in data files rather than embedding all values directly into the combat engine.

This will allow balancing without rewriting game logic.

## 32. Performance Requirements

The engine should support large battles with many simultaneous units.

Implement:

- Efficient nearby-target queries
- Batched rendering
- Projectile pooling
- Limited collision checks
- Lightweight autonomous movement
- Efficient destruction and cleanup of dead units
- Caps on temporary summons
- Avoidance of unnecessary simulation updates for inactive objects

Target smooth gameplay with several hundred active units.

A recommended initial performance target is 60 FPS rendering on a typical desktop with 300–500 active units, subject to hardware and browser capability.

Avoid implementing full general-purpose RTS pathfinding when the map only requires movement through three fixed lanes.

Use lane paths, local steering, and lightweight collision avoidance.

## 33. Multiplayer Architecture

The long-term target is online 3v3 multiplayer.

Use a server-authoritative model.

The server should control:

- Currency balances
- Valid building placement
- Unit spawning
- Combat
- Beacon capture
- Research
- Investments
- Victory conditions

Clients should submit player decisions rather than authoritative unit positions.

This prevents clients from directly modifying resources or combat results.

The game should support:

- Public or private lobbies
- Team assignment
- Faction selection
- Match start synchronization
- Reconnection
- Player departure handling
- Team victory and defeat

### Player departures

Do not automatically divide a departing player's income among remaining teammates in a way that creates a significant economic exploit.

For the first implementation, replace disconnected players with AI using the same resources and assets they possessed at the time of departure.

Players should be able to reconnect and resume control.

# PART VII — TESTING AND BALANCING

## 34. Combat Testing

Create a combat simulation test harness that can evaluate army compositions without requiring a human to play an entire match.

Every test should measure:

- Units spawned
- Units killed
- Damage dealt
- Damage received
- Surviving units
- Time to defeat the enemy
- Resource cost of each production composition
- Resulting beacon control
- Headquarters damage

### Required tests

**Mirror tests**

Identical armies in symmetrical positions should win approximately equally over a range of seeds.

**Basic counter tests**

Splash-heavy armies should perform better against clustered infantry than comparable single-target armies.

**Anti-air tests**

Air units should strongly punish unsupported ground-only armies, while appropriate anti-air compositions should counter them.

**Frontline tests**

Ranged armies with adequate frontline protection should often perform better than equivalent unprotected ranged armies.

**Support tests**

Healing and shielding should provide meaningful benefits without allowing permanent stalemates.

**Elite tests**

Tier 4 units should be powerful but should not automatically defeat equal-cost counter-compositions.

## 35. Economy Testing

Create automated simulations for different economic strategies.

Examples:

- Never invest; purchase only military production.
- Invest as soon as two beacons are controlled.
- Invest only with three beacons.
- Research technology as early as possible.
- Delay research to overwhelm the opponent.
- Build a balanced combination of military units and investments.

Observe:

- Total income
- Investment payback
- Average military strength
- Territorial control
- Win rates
- Match duration

The goal is not to make every strategy equally strong in every situation.

The goal is to ensure that different strategies become effective under different battlefield conditions.

## 36. Match-Level Balance Targets

Suggested initial targets:

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Standard match duration | 15–25 minutes |
| Balanced-faction win rates | Approximately 45–55% across sufficient tests |
| Identical mirror matchups | Near 50/50 |
| First investment payback with 2 beacons | Around 3 minutes |
| First investment payback with 3 beacons | Around 2 minutes |
| Tier 4 impact | Significant but counterable |
| Combat advantage from appropriate counters | Meaningful, not absolute |
| Long-match performance | Stable with several hundred units |

Automated results should inform adjustments.

Do not automatically change a unit merely because it wins a single test.

Consider its performance across multiple matchups and complete games.

## 37. Gameplay Acceptance Tests

Before considering the prototype finished, verify the following:

- [ ] A player can select and place a production building.
- [ ] The purchase correctly reduces currency.
- [ ] The building automatically spawns units every 30 seconds.
- [ ] A unit's spawn position reflects its building position.
- [ ] Units independently advance down their assigned lane.
- [ ] Units automatically find and attack valid enemies.
- [ ] Ground-only units cannot attack air units.
- [ ] Dead units are removed correctly.
- [ ] Surviving units remain alive between waves.
- [ ] Units capture resource beacons by crossing them.
- [ ] Beacon ownership is displayed correctly.
- [ ] Beacon ownership correctly changes income.
- [ ] Players receive recurring base income.
- [ ] Lane investments increase income only from controlled beacons.
- [ ] Research consumes resources and takes time.
- [ ] Newly researched production buildings become available.
- [ ] Tier 4 production obeys the one-token restriction.
- [ ] Players can sell buildings.
- [ ] Special abilities activate, deal their intended effects, and respect cooldowns.
- [ ] AI players can complete a match without human intervention.
- [ ] Headquarters take damage from attacking units.
- [ ] A match correctly ends when a headquarters is destroyed.
- [ ] All three lanes work independently.
- [ ] Allied players can support each other's lanes.
- [ ] The simulation remains stable through extended matches.
