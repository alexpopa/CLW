# Game design (current build: 1v1, 2v2 and 3v3, one lane per player, four unit tiers)

Working title: **Gravemarch**. This describes what is implemented. For the original game and the long-term plan see the [Colonial Line Wars research and recreation specification](COLONIAL_LINE_WARS.md); this build is [Phase 1](COLONIAL_LINE_WARS.md#38-phase-1--functional-core) of it plus the unit roster, research and abilities of the later phases. Every unit, its ability, its counters and how balance is tested are in [UNITS.md](UNITS.md). The shared [project rules](../AGENTS.md) govern implementation.

All numbers below live in `packages/sim/src/data.ts` and started from Part III of the specification. They are original values, not statistics from any existing game.

## Core loop
A match is 1v1, 2v2 or 3v3, chosen on the title screen, with **one lane per player of a team**: one, two or three parallel lanes, each 480 units long and 24 deep. Team 0's cathedral is at the left end, team 1's at the right; a team has a single cathedral behind all of its lanes, with 4,500 health per lane. Each player has a home lane (where his keepers start and his tithe applies) but may build in any lane of his side. In a solo match the player takes the first seat and the autopilot plays every other seat, allies included.

Every lane has its own building ground, three shrines, two guard towers and two cathedral bastions per side. Units stay in the lane they were mustered in. Lanes are drawn far to near, separated by strips of graveyard behind an iron fence; a gate in the wall and a paved walk join neighbouring building grounds, and the camera scrolls along and across the lanes.
1. **Gravekeepers build chantries.** Each player has two gravekeepers, the **Sexton** and the **Undertaker**, who walk the building ground: a paved precinct 36 long and 24 wide (room for 216 chantries) that runs from the cathedral steps to the base towers, walled along its sides only. Nothing stands between it and the cathedral: an enemy who breaks through marches across the ground and strikes the cathedral itself, or, in a lane that ends beside it, the chapel of the cathedral close that closes that lane. They never step onto the lane, cannot fight and cannot be harmed. To build, choose a keeper, choose a unit and click the ground: the gold is paid at once, the keeper walks there, and the chantry stands the moment he arrives (there is no construction time; it musters from the next wave). Orders queue, up to eight per keeper. Sending a keeper somewhere else cancels his queue and returns the gold, and an order whose ground has been taken by the time he arrives is refunded. A chantry covers 2 × 2 cells of a hidden 1-unit grid, so neighbours can be offset from each other; nothing may overlap, including chantries on order. The Sexton raises tiers I to III. The Undertaker raises every tier, is the only one who can raise tier IV, and hires mercenaries.
2. **Waves.** Every 30 s (one global timer) each finished chantry musters its unit (a kennel musters two hounds) where it stands, so placement decides formation: front columns reach the fight first.
3. **Units fight on their own.** They march toward the enemy cathedral, engage the nearest enemy in reach, and attack the cathedral when nothing else is in the way. There is no unit control.
4. **Shrines.** Three checkpoints sit at 25 %, 50 % and 75 % of the lane. Each is drawn as a pair of shrines flanking the lane, one on either edge, so nothing stands in the army's path; it is still one checkpoint, held by whoever's units stand between the pair. Each side starts the match holding the shrine nearest its cathedral (its home shrine); the middle one is neutral. A shrine flips to a team whose units stand on it uncontested. Each shrine held pays income.
5. **Guard towers and bastions.** Two guard towers (2,400 HP, armor 2, 24 damage every 0.6 s, range 9) stand on each home shrine. Two **cathedral bastions**, larger and stronger (3,600 HP, armor 3, 26 damage every 0.6 s, range 10), stand at the front of each building ground, near the top and bottom edges of the lane, as the cathedral's last wall; which kind stands where is `MAP.shrineTower` / `MAP.baseTower`. Both shoot attackers, and advancing units stop to fight them: an army has to bring down the bastions before it can reach the chantries and the cathedral. The home shrine cannot be captured while either of its towers stands. A fallen tower is gone for the match.
6. **Income** every 10 s: `32 + shrines held × (4 + tithe bonus)`.
7. **Tithe** (lane investment): every level costs 300 and adds +8 gold per held shrine (owner decisions: the price never climbs, and there is no highest level). Never refunded. Worth it when you hold two or more shrines. The bar's button and 3 raise it on your own lane; a plus button beside each lane of the minimap raises it on that lane, your own or an ally's.
8. **Research.** You begin knowing three tier I units. Everything else must be researched, one research at a time:
   - **Study** a unit of an open tier to unlock its chantry: 90 gold and 12 s for a tier I unit, 150 gold and 18 s for tier II, 260 gold and 25 s for tier III.
   - **The Second Rite** (400 gold, 30 s) opens tier II and **the Third Rite** (800 gold, 45 s) opens tier III. A rite unlocks no unit by itself: each of the tier's eight units still needs its own study.

   - **The Fourth Rite** (1,500 gold, 60 s) opens tier IV; each of its four units costs 450 gold and 35 s to study.

   There are 26 units (6 + 8 + 8 + 4), so nobody can afford to learn everything early: what to study first, and when to stop building and pay for a rite, is the main strategic choice.
9. **Mercenaries.** The Undertaker hires bands for a single payment (90 to 210 gold, six seconds between hirings). A band appears in front of the base towers of the lane he is in, fights whatever it meets, and leaves when it reaches the middle of the lane. Mercenaries are for holding your half or retaking the middle shrine; they never reach the enemy.
10. **Selling** a chantry refunds 75 % before its first wave and 25 % after.
11. **Victory**: the enemy cathedral (4,500 HP, with a weak defensive attack) reaches zero. After 25 minutes sudden death begins: cathedrals stop defending themselves, take triple damage, and crumble by 8 health a second for each of (1 + shrines the enemy holds), so a match always ends within about ten more minutes and holding ground decides a deadlock.

12. **Storm of Wrath.** A player who has completed The Third Rite may call the storm (5): 500 gold, then 150 seconds before he may call it again. For 10 seconds lightning strikes every enemy soldier on the whole board, in every lane, on the ground and in the air, for 9 damage a second each (90 in all), ignoring armor. Towers and cathedrals are not harmed. Every soldier takes the same, so it thins swarms and softens big units but does not kill a tier III army; a tier I Arbalester (80 health) dies to a full storm, an Iron Penitent barely notices. Storms stack: allies calling together, or both sides at once, each strike in full. The numbers are `storm*` in `RULES`; the autopilot calls it when at least 16 enemy soldiers are on the board and it has gold to spare.
13. **Deadly Fog.** Every player's last resort (6): free, with no tier needed, and **once a match** for each player. A killing fog pours out of the caster's cathedral and rolls down one lane (the caster's own by default; the client sends the lane in the middle of his view, so in team games he can save an ally's). Its front moves at a steady pace and after 18 seconds reaches halfway between the home shrine and the middle shrine (180 from the lane end); it stands 5 seconds more and lifts. Every enemy soldier inside it dies at once, whatever its tier, on the ground or in the air, including anything that marches into it while it stands. Towers and cathedrals are not harmed, and the caster's side is untouched. The numbers are `fog*` in `RULES`; the autopilot calls it down its own lane when ten enemy soldiers stand where it will roll, or any at all once the cathedral is below half health. The fog is drawn as four stacked sheets of drifting, warped noise (two on a struggling phone), so the army inside stays visible.
14. **Ossuary Vultures.** A player who has completed The Second Rite may send them (0): free, then 120 seconds before he may send them again. They have **no button and no timer in the HUD** (owner decision); a phone shows one plain button in the touch bar, since it has no keys. Two carrion birds of bone and funeral cloth rise from his side's home shrine in **the lane his Sexton is standing in** and fly abreast, without ever stopping, to the enemy's home shrine, where they come apart and are gone (24 seconds). They are not units: nothing can target or hurt them. Each bird lets a skull urn fall on the nearest enemy ground soldier within 10 of it, at most one urn every 2 seconds; the urn lands 0.7 seconds later where he stood and deals 110 damage, through armor, to every enemy ground soldier within 6. Flyers, towers and cathedrals are spared, so a quick foe can step out from under an urn and wings ignore them. The numbers are `vulture*` in `RULES`; the autopilot sends them whenever five enemy ground soldiers are in its Sexton's lane.

## Units
Six tier I, eight tier II, eight tier III and four tier IV units, each with an automatic ability, plus four mercenary bands. The full tables, the counter relationships and the balance method are in [UNITS.md](UNITS.md). In short:

- **Tier I:** Arbalester, Oathguard, Pyre Zealot (known from the start); Grave Hounds, Flagellant, Hexbinder.
- **Tier II:** Plague Doctor, Dreadknight, Gargoyle (flies), Witch Hunter, Iron Penitent, Necromancer, Trebuchet, Shade.
- **Tier III:** Bone Colossus, Ash Wyrm (flies), Tempest Cantor, Reliquary, Crimson Countess, Impaler, Pale Lich, Headsman.
- **Tier IV (Undertaker only):** Belfry Titan, Ashen Seraph (flies), Pale Rider, Hellmouth.
- **Mercenaries (hired, not built):** Gallowglass, Free Marksmen, Fire-Eater, Shackled Ogre.

Combat rules:
- Damage taken = `max(1, damage − armor)`. Some abilities ignore armor, strip it, or add to it.
- **Ground and air.** Flying units can only be attacked by units whose attack reaches the air (the unit tables say which). A unit that cannot hit anything in front of it marches on. Flyers do not hold shrines. Towers and cathedrals strike both.
- Units choose the nearest enemy they are able to attack and keep it while it stays close.
- Everyone decides from where all units stood at the start of the tick; damage, healing, afflictions and movement are then applied together. Neither side ever acts first, and a mirrored match stays exactly mirrored (tested).
- Units of the same team keep a little spacing so lines form; flyers only crowd other flyers.
- Most units cross the lane at 6 units per second. Hounds, cavalry, the Shade and the Countess are faster; war engines and the largest units are slower.

## AI
`simpleAI(state, player)` in `packages/sim/src/ai.ts`. Stateless and rule-abiding. Each match the seed gives each autopilot one of four ways to play (`AI.profiles` in `data.ts`): *host of the dead* (Flagellants, Iron Penitents, Necromancers, then the Bone Colossus), *hunt and sky* (Hounds, Dreadknights, Gargoyles, then the Ash Wyrm), *siege line* (Hexbinders, Trebuchets, the Impaler under a Reliquary) and *blood court* (Shades, Dreadknights, then the Countess and the Headsman). A profile is a research order and a target army mix. The autopilot defends when enemies pass its nearest shrine, researches the next step of its order once it has enough chantries, builds anti-air when the enemy fields flyers, raises the tithe when it holds two shrines, and otherwise builds toward its mix. It gives each of its gravekeepers one order at a time, uses the Undertaker for tier IV, and hires mercenaries when enemies are past its nearest shrine and it has gold to spare. It is an opponent for practice and a test vehicle, not a challenge for an experienced player.

## Presentation
Gothic dark fantasy. Everything is original and generated at load time: low-poly 3D models built from primitives (`apps/client/src/models.ts`), procedural textures (`textures.ts`) and synthesized sound (`audio.ts`). There are no downloaded art or audio assets. Soldiers and chantries are drawn small against the lane so large armies and dense building ground stay readable. Units are rigged, not rigid: each has a gait (walk, shuffle, gallop, lope, stomp, float, roll, fly), turns toward its target, leans into its stride, lunges when it strikes and breathes when it stands, and carries separately moving pieces (capes, wings, tails, hanging chains and lanterns, wheels, orbiting ice). Each attack has its own effect and sound: bolts, gunshots, curses, frost bursts, chain lightning, fire breath, the Impaler's stake. The camera is a fixed elevated RTS view that scrolls horizontally along the lane.

The bar always shows the two gravekeepers. The build menu appears only while one is selected, and shows one tab at a time: tiers I to III for the Sexton, one tab each; the Undertaker has a single page, tier IV on the top row (Q W E R) and the mercenaries under it (A S D F). Switching keeper switches the page. A card you know is picked for building; a card of an open tier you have not studied starts its study when clicked and shows its progress; cards of a sealed tier show the tier numeral; a mercenary card hires the band. A selected keeper has a ring under him, his queued chantries are drawn as ghost footprints, and hovering a card shows the unit's ability, statistics and what it can strike.

**The Sexton's teleport.** Press T (which also selects him), then click any ground of your side, in any lane, in the world or on the minimap: he is there at once, as often as you like (no rest between steps). Any chantries he had on order are cancelled and refunded. The Undertaker has to walk, crossing from one lane's ground to the next through the gate.

**Minimap.** A map of the whole battlefield sits in the bottom-right corner: every lane with its building grounds, shrines, towers, armies, your keepers (bright) and your allies' (dim), and a frame for what the screen shows. Click or drag on it to look there.

Controls: 1 selects the Sexton and 2 the Undertaker (or click them); T arms the Sexton's teleport; the cards of the shown tab sit in two staggered rows like the keys that pick, study or hire them, Q W E R above and A S D F below (with no keeper chosen, the one who can raise it is chosen for you); Z X C switch between the Sexton's tabs (tiers I, II, III), as does Tab; 3 tithe on your own lane (the plus beside each lane of the minimap raises it there), 4 rite, 5 the Storm of Wrath, 6 the Deadly Fog (once a match), 0 the Ossuary Vultures; left-click ground of your side to place the chosen unit (keep clicking to queue more) or, with no unit chosen, to send the keeper walking; right-click cancels placing or aiming, or sends the keeper walking; Esc backs out one step; click a chantry then Delete to sell; drag, the arrow keys, the screen edges or the minimap to scroll (the wheel scrolls across the lanes, shift-wheel along them; with one lane it scrolls along); Space returns to your own ground; P pause.

**Touch (phones, iPhone included).** One finger drags the view, two pinch it nearer or farther; a tap does what a click does. Nothing depends on hovering: a card that would spend gold at once (a study, a hiring) first opens its note with a Study or Hire button, and a second tap pays. Three buttons above the bar stand in for the keys: Teleport, Cathedral (look home) and Cancel (back out one step). On a phone the bar is compact, the cards are one row that slides sideways, the map moves to the top right, and the layout keeps clear of the notch and the home bar. Held upright the bar takes two rows; on its side is the better way to play.

**The dock.** The note about whatever is pointed at always sits directly above the bar, so it can never be hidden behind it.

## Online play
The title screen offers "Host a match" and "Join" next to the solo game, for the team size chosen there. The host gets a four-letter code. Players who join are seated side by side (the first joiner is an opponent, the next an ally, and so on); the match starts the moment every seat is taken, or the host can begin at once and the server's autopilot takes the empty seats. Players are guests who pick a display name; there are no accounts yet. The match server (`apps/server`) owns the match: it applies each player's commands, runs the autopilot for any empty seat, and sends every client the exact commands of each tick to replay on its own copy of the sim. A player who leaves is replaced by the autopilot. Pause and game speed are solo-only. Not yet: choosing your seat or team in the lobby, reconnecting to a match in progress, spectators, rankings.

## Not in this build
The three factions, player-cast abilities, lanes that interact (units never change lane), accounts in the client, hosted multiplayer. These are Phases 2–4 of the specification.
